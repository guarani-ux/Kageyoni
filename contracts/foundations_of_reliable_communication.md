# Foundations of Reliable Human Communication
## An Evidence Base for an AI-Assisted Communication and Creative-Production System

### 1. Research Basis and Epistemic Stance

Communication is treated as a chain of **transformations under uncertainty** rather than as a collection of writing techniques. Reliable communication depends on maintaining the distinctions and relationships that allow evidence to become warranted understanding, understanding to become purposeful composition, composition to survive representation in a medium, and audience response to be evaluated without confusing proxies with outcomes.

> **Foundational Axiom: A statement occurring in a source is not thereby a fact.**

### 2. The Nine Epistemic Status Categories

| Status | Operational Meaning | What Would Make It Unsafe to Promote It |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SOURCE-SUPPLIED FACT** | A proposition explicitly reported by an identifiable source (a source assertion presented as factual). | Treating the source's assertion as independently established simply because it was written down. |
| **EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED CLAIM** | A proposition for which relevant evidence provides identifiable warrant, calibrated to bias, consistency, directness, and precision. | Suppressing contrary evidence, indirectness, missing data, or uncertainty. |
| **INFERENCE** | A proposition derived from other propositions through an identifiable reasoning step. | Rewriting the conclusion so that the reasoning step disappears. |
| **ASSUMPTION** | A proposition provisionally taken as true without sufficient warrant. | Allowing later artifacts to inherit it without the assumption marker. |
| **HEURISTIC** | A practical rule of thumb that may improve decisions in recurring circumstances. | Converting frequency or usefulness into necessity. |
| **INTERPRETATION** | A context-dependent account of what evidence, language, or creative material means. | Presenting one plausible reading as entailed when alternatives remain viable. |
| **CREATIVE DECISION** | A deliberate selection among viable expressive possibilities in service of an objective. | Describing the selected option as factually demanded by evidence. |
| **PREFERENCE** | A stakeholder, organizational, cultural, or aesthetic valuation. | Converting authority or taste into an empirical claim. |
| **UNKNOWN** | Evidence is absent, inaccessible, contradictory, or too weak. | Filling the gap with a plausible completion and forgetting it was invented. |

### 4. Ten Invariant Principles of Communication Physics

1. **Epistemic Laundering Prohibition:** A statement supplied by a source must remain distinguishable from an independently warranted conclusion.
2. **Provenance Is Not Truth:** Derivation, agents, activities, and revisions allow traceability, but perfect lineage can trace a false statement perfectly.
3. **Calibrated Confidence:** Confidence must be attached to conclusions, not inferred from tone, eloquence, or source prestige.
4. **Relational Comprehension:** Understanding requires the construction of relations and a situation model, not merely the accumulation of isolated sentences.
5. **Surface Cohesion $\neq$ Global Coherence:** Fluent transitions, repeated pronouns, and connectors can create cohesion while remaining globally incoherent.
6. **Audience Common Ground:** Omitted information can be coherent for one audience and incomprehensible for another based on shared knowledge.
7. **Expertise Reversal:** Explanations and guidance beneficial to novices can become redundant or harmful to experts.
8. **No Universal Pacing Formula:** There is no universal 3-second attention rule or 3-act story necessity; attention and suspense depend on goal-driven change and anticipated uncertainty.
9. **Audiovisual Asynchrony:** Auditory and visual channels interact; words should not duplicate what the eyes can already see.
10. **Proxy Substitution Prohibition:** Readability is not comprehension; clicks are not persuasion; post-hoc correlation is not causal impact.
